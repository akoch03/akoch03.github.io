DWG 01 — INTERACTIVE MODELS

Flex Body Optimization — GM Capstone

SPONSOR: GENERAL MOTORS · ME 481 · JAN–APR 2026
NX → ABAQUS → SIMPACK

Full-vehicle multibody models with 17+ flexible components are accurate — and too slow for routine design iteration. Our five-person team built GM a set of best practices for choosing exactly how much flexibility each suspension component actually needs, per event type.

I modeled the front suspension and rear axle in NX to GM's hardpoints and mass targets, generated Craig-Bampton reduced flex bodies in Abaqus, and integrated them into a full-vehicle Simpack model. We then ran a structured DoE across ride, handling, and durability events comparing three eigenmode allocation strategies.

KEY RESULT Our Component Sensitivity method was 8× more accurate than spreading flexibility evenly across components — at the same computational cost.
  • METHODS DEVELOPEDModal Participation & Component Sensitivity distribution
  • RECOMMENDATIONSEvent-specific flexibility settings for ride, handling & durability

Bottle Line Accumulation — Drop-Down Rail

PERRIGO — PRODUCTION ENGINEERING INTERN · SUMMER 2025
TO-SCALE LINE REPLICA — SIEMENS NX

A to-scale CAD replica of a live bottle-packaging production line, built to sell a floor change before anyone touched the floor.

The accumulation table needed to run a single-file line of bottles and switch between modes without stopping production. My solution: a drop-down rail that lowers into the lane to channel bottles into a single line, then retracts for full-width accumulation — a seamless changeover with no manual rework between runs.

WHY MODEL IT The replica was the visual aid that carried the proposal — operators and engineering could walk the change in 3D, catch clearance issues, and sign off before fabrication.
  • SCOPEFull line layout: conveyors, accumulation table, rail mechanism
  • MECHANISMRetractable drop-down guide rail for single-line / bulk switching
  • OUTCOMEImplemented solution communicated & validated through the model
